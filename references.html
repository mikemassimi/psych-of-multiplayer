03 — Social Loafing Effort

  • Latané, B., Williams, K., & Harkins, S. (1979). Many hands make light the work: The causes and consequences of social loafing. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology. DOI
  • Suleiman, J., & Watson, R. T. (2008). Social loafing in technology-supported teams. Computer Supported Cooperative Work. DOI Loafing increases in remote/tech-mediated teams via reduced visibility — the human-only baseline.
  • Stieglitz, S., Mirbabaie, M., Möllmann, N. R. J., & Rzyski, J. (2021). Collaborating with Virtual Assistants in Organizations. Information Systems Frontiers. DOI Direct evidence that employees cede responsibility and loaf when paired with a virtual assistant.

  • Shehata, D., & Li, M. (2026). The Bystander Effect in Multi-Agent Reasoning. arXiv. arXiv The bystander effect also occurs among agents themselves.

  • Lau, D. C., & Murnighan, J. K. (1998). Demographic diversity and faultlines: The compositional dynamics of organizational groups. Academy of Management Review. DOI

06 — Transactive Memory Structure

  • Wegner, D. M. (1987). Transactive memory: A contemporary analysis of the group mind. In B. Mullen & G. R. Goethals (Eds.), Theories of Group Behavior. PDF
  • Massimi, M., et al. (2024). It's a Hot Spaghetti Mess: Knowledge management in Slack. Slack internal research. Slides Slack is a way to get ahold of people who have answers, not a knowledge repository. Users find “knowledge magnets” who reheat cold information through conversation.
  • Bienefeld, N., Kolbe, M., Camen, G., Huser, D., et al. (2023). Transactive memory and speaking up in human-AI teams. Frontiers in Psychology. DOI Field study of 180 ICU personnel, not a lab study.
  • Duan, W., Zhou, S., Scalia, M. J., et al. (2026). “If They Don’t Even Trust Their AI Teammate, Why Should We?” Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact. DOI Trust contagion spreading across human subgroups.

Additional related work

  • Woolley, A. W., Chabris, C. F., Pentland, A., Hashmi, N., & Malone, T. W. (2010). Evidence for a Collective Intelligence Factor in the Performance of Human Groups. Science. DOI Collective intelligence is driven by social sensitivity and turn-taking equality, not average individual intelligence.
  • Tajfel, H., & Turner, J. C. (1979). An integrative theory of intergroup conflict. In W. G. Austin & S. Worchel (Eds.), The Social Psychology of Intergroup Relations. Google Scholar Social Identity Theory — do agents become an out-group that increases human in-group cohesion?
  • Ren, Y., Kraut, R., & Kiesler, S. (2007). Applying common identity and bond theory to design of online communities. Organization Studies. DOI Does an agent read as part of a channel's identity or compete with bond-based attachment?
  • Clark, H. H., & Brennan, S. E. (1991). Grounding in communication. In L. B. Resnick et al. (Eds.), Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition. DOI
  • Roberts, T. L., Lowry, P. B., et al. (2006). Impact of social presence through group size and collaborative software on group member voice. IEEE Transactions on Professional Communication. DOI As group size grows, participation becomes more unequal.
  • Song, T., Tan, Y., Zhu, Z., Feng, Y., & Lee, Y.-C. (2025). Multi-Agents are Social Groups. Proc. ACM Hum.-Comput. Interact. DOI Agent count alone changes social influence — multiple agents increased felt social pressure.
  • Wang, Y., Lei, K., Chiu, S.-Y., Isbister, K., Lee, D. T., et al. (2026). SeeSawBot: An LLM-Driven Chatbot Mediating Across Private and Shared Slack Channels. CHI. DOI Slack-specific deployment; the closest existing artifact to this exact design space.
  • Yeo, S. Y., Zhang, T., Bateman, S., Hsieh, G., et al. (2026). Group Conversational Agents: A Review. ACM DIS. DOI Review of 53 studies; names three design tensions: visibility vs. discretion, proactivity vs. group autonomy, agent authority vs. group ownership.
  • Houde, S., Brimijoin, K., Muller, M., Ross, S. I., et al. (2025). Controlling AI Agent Participation in Group Conversations. ACM IUI. DOI Groups wanted explicit controls over when, what, and where an agent speaks.
  • Yan, L., Han, X., Zhang, Y., Greiff, S., Molenaar, I., et al. (2025). The Social Blindspot in Human-AI Collaboration. arXiv. arXiv An AI presence reshapes coordination among the human teammates, even when undetected.
  • Emich, K. J., Bell, B. S., & Hollenbeck, J. R., et al. (2026). 40 Questions for team science. Personnel Psychology. DOI Names faultlines and transactive memory as still-open questions in team science.
  • Postmes, T., Spears, R., & Lea, M. (1998). Breaching or building social boundaries? SIDE-effects of computer-mediated communication. Communication Research. DOI Foundational SIDE model paper; low-cue environments amplify group identity effects.
  • Seering, J., Ng, F., Yao, Z., & Kaufman, G. (2018). Applications of social identity theory to research and design in CSCW. CHI. DOI The closest precedent for applying SIT/SIDE to CSCW product design.
